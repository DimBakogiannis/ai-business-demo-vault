## Google Debuts Fitbit Air: Affordable Screenless Health Tracking Enters Competitive Market

**Google has unveiled the Fitbit Air, a minimalist wearable priced at $100 that directly challenges devices like Whoop by eliminating the display while maintaining comprehensive health monitoring capabilities.** The screenless device tracks 24/7 heart rate data, heart rhythm monitoring with atrial fibrillation alerts, blood oxygen levels, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, and sleep stages with automatic activity recognition. The Fitbit Air weighs just 12 grams with its band and is 25% smaller than the Fitbit Luxe and 50% smaller than the Inspire 3, according to TechCrunch.

The design philosophy centers on allowing users to "live in the moment" without constant screen distractions, while all tracking data syncs to the rebranded Google Health app—the successor to the traditional Fitbit application. Google positions the device for consumers who find existing wearables too bulky, complicated, or expensive. The wearable offers impressive durability with water resistance up to 50 meters and delivers a week of battery life on a single charge, with fast charging capable of providing a full day of power in just five minutes.

The company is launching three band options—a Performance Loop Band made from recycled materials, a waterproof Active Band, and a discreet Elevated Modern Band—to appeal to different usage preferences. Notably, the Fitbit Air integrates with the Pixel Watch ecosystem, allowing users to switch between devices depending on context. Pre-orders are now available with shipments beginning May 26.

This launch represents Google's strategic pivot toward health as a core service offering, strengthened by the simultaneous introduction of Google Health Coach, a Gemini-powered AI assistant that serves as a personalized fitness trainer and wellness advisor for premium subscribers. The Fitbit Air establishes Google in direct competition with specialized fitness brands while leveraging its AI capabilities to differentiate from traditional wearable manufacturers.

## Apple's MacBook Neo Budget Model Threatened by Rising RAM Costs

**Apple's $599 MacBook Neo may lose its most compelling feature—its aggressive pricing—as rising RAM shortages force the company to consider discontinuing the entry-level configuration.** According to analyst Tim Culpan cited by The Verge, Apple is evaluating whether to eliminate the base $599 model and offer only the $699 variant with 512GB of storage, mirroring similar moves made to the Mac mini and Mac Studio product lines in recent months.

The timing presents an ironic twist: the MacBook Neo's tremendous market success—currently experiencing a two to three-week delivery delay—is partly responsible for the supply pressure. The device arrived just as global RAM shortages escalated costs for manufacturers, and its popularity has driven Apple to double production targets from 5-6 million to 10 million units annually. The initial production run leveraged surplus A18 Pro chips from iPhone 16 Pro manufacturing, but expanding production requires ordering additional processors specifically for the Neo, compounding cost pressures.

Apple faces a production dilemma that resembles earlier challenges with component shortages. The first batch of MacBook Neos benefited from existing chip inventory and tighter cost controls, but subsequent production runs will necessarily be more expensive due to both dedicated A18 Pro chip manufacturing and escalating component costs industry-wide. The Verge notes that Apple has already discontinued lower-storage configurations across multiple product lines, suggesting a potential pricing strategy adjustment for the Neo.

This scenario illustrates how supply chain disruptions can undermine Apple's competitive positioning in the budget segment. While competitors struggle with higher overall costs, eliminating the entry-level MacBook Neo would remove the primary incentive driving adoption—making the device less accessible to first-time and price-conscious buyers who drove its initial momentum.

## Skyroot Becomes India's First Space Tech Unicorn as Rocket Launch Nears

**Indian rocket startup Skyroot Aerospace achieved unicorn status with a $1.1 billion pre-money valuation after securing $60 million in fresh funding, positioning the company for its maiden orbital launch within weeks.** The funding round, detailed on TechCrunch, included $50 million in primary equity co-led by Sherpalo Ventures and GIC, complemented by approximately $10 million in structured debt from BlackRock-affiliated funds. Ram Shriram, founder of Sherpalo Ventures and an Alphabet board member, will join Skyroot's board of directors.

Founded in 2018 by former Indian Space Research Organization engineers Pawan Kumar Chandana and Naga Bharath Daka, Skyroot is developing small satellite launch vehicles comparable to those produced by U.S. companies Rocket Lab and Firefly Aerospace. The Vikram-1 rocket, currently positioned at India's Sriharikota spaceport, is designed to deliver payloads up to 350 kilograms into low Earth orbit. Skyroot targets a June launch following completion of flight qualification tests and integration activities.

The valuation more than doubled from the $500 million pre-money figure achieved during the company's previous funding round in 2023, reflecting accelerating global investor confidence in India's emerging private space sector. Skyroot declined to disclose revenue or customer backlog details but noted that demand for dedicated small satellite launch services remains robust, with approximately one-third of projected demand originating domestically and two-thirds from international customers.

This milestone signals India's broader ambition to expand its share of the global space economy through private sector participation. India's space economy, currently valued at $8.4 billion, is projected to reach $44 billion by 2033, with nearly 400 space-tech startups operating as of early 2026. Skyroot's achievement follows policy reforms since 2020 that granted private companies access to ISRO facilities and participation in complete space activities, while complementing state-run ISRO operations that have experienced recent setbacks.

## Toronto Police Arrest SMS Blaster Operators in Canada's First Known Case

**Toronto police have arrested three men and filed 44 charges in connection with operating an SMS blaster—a cell tower-spoofing device that compromised tens of thousands of devices through malicious text message campaigns.** The Toronto Police Service confirmed this represents the "first known instance" of SMS blaster activity in Canada, marking a significant escalation in mobile network security threats. The scheme operated for several months beginning in November 2025, targeting personal and banking credentials through phishing messages.

SMS blasters exploit legacy 2G cellular network security weaknesses by emitting stronger signals than legitimate cell towers, forcing nearby devices to connect to the malicious infrastructure. Once connected, the blaster broadcasts thousands of text messages containing fraudulent links impersonating legitimate businesses' login pages. According to Detective Sergeant Lindsay Riddell, the Toronto operation specifically sought to steal usernames, passwords, and banking credentials from unsuspecting users. The devices also interfere with cellular communications infrastructure and can disrupt emergency 911 services.

Authorities revealed the SMS blaster was "uniquely built" and mobile, operating from a vehicle that traveled across multiple Toronto locations to conduct the attacks. Police declined to release images of the confiscated device, citing safety concerns. The investigation parallels a 2024 case in Thailand where gang members operated a similar SMS blaster from a truck, broadcasting nearly one million messages over three days.

The arrest highlights growing vulnerabilities in older cellular infrastructure and the practical risks posed by deliberate technology misuse. Security experts recommend users disable 2G connectivity on their devices to mitigate SMS blaster risks. Apple users can activate Lockdown Mode to automatically switch off 2G radios, representing a proactive defense against tower-spoofing attacks. This case underscores the importance of network modernization and mobile security awareness as telecommunications infrastructure modernizes globally.
