# Today's Top Tech: AI Health Coaching, Voice-Powered Streaming, and Mobile Security Concerns

The tech landscape continues to shift as major platforms integrate artificial intelligence into consumer services while confronting emerging security challenges. ## Google Launches Premium AI Health Coach Powered by Gemini

Google has officially launched Google Health Coach, a subscription-based artificial intelligence service that combines the functions of a personal fitness trainer, sleep specialist, and wellness advisor. The service, powered by Google's Gemini AI platform, is now available to Google Health Premium subscribers at $9.99 per month or $99 annually, with premium access included for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. The rollout marks a significant expansion of Google's health ecosystem, which has grown substantially since its 2021 acquisition of Fitbit.

The Health Coach functions as an interactive digital advisor that learns user preferences through an initial onboarding process where subscribers specify fitness goals, daily routines, available exercise equipment, existing injuries, and lifestyle factors. Once configured, the system provides personalized guidance through natural language interactions, allowing users to update their information, log workouts and meals through voice dictation, and upload health documents and photos. The system synthesizes data from multiple sources including fitness metrics, sleep patterns, nutrition tracking, environmental factors, and linked medical records to generate comprehensive insights rather than simple summaries.

According to TechCrunch, the AI coach integrates throughout the Google Health app interface, informing workout suggestions in the Fitness tab, sleep guidance in the Sleep tab, and metric summaries in the Health tab. The feature will initially roll out to Fitbit and Pixel Watch users beginning May 19, with broader device support coming later. Google has redesigned cycle tracking, nutrition, and mental well-being features specifically to complement the coach's capabilities. The company notes that users without compatible wearables can download the Google Health app and will receive notifications when the coach becomes available to them.

**Why it matters:** This launch positions Google directly against specialized health platforms and positions artificial intelligence as the central organizing principle for personalized health guidance. By bundling the coach with its Fitbit ecosystem and AI subscription tiers, Google creates multiple revenue streams while leveraging its AI expertise to differentiate its health offerings. The move also signals a shift from generic fitness tracking toward AI-driven behavioral coaching, a space previously dominated by specialized startups and expensive personal training services.

## Netflix Deploys Native AI Voice Search in Breakthrough Move

Netflix has begun testing an AI-powered voice search feature that allows subscribers to find content through natural language requests directly within the Netflix application. The feature, currently available to select subscribers in the United States, circumvents the voice assistants built into smart TV platforms by embedding intelligence directly into Netflix's streaming interface. Users access the feature by pressing the Netflix button on their remote, selecting suggested search categories like "I need a good cry," or tapping an "Ask" button to initiate voice-powered recommendations.

According to The Verge's testing, the voice search demonstrates impressive performance on both obvious and obscure requests. Simple queries like "date night movies from the '80s" surface classics such as The Breakfast Club, while highly specific requests like "I like the music of Brian Eno, what should I watch" result in appropriate recommendations including Abstract: The Art of Design. The system even handles emotionally nuanced prompts effectively, suggesting Raising Dion and A Series of Unfortunate Events in response to "fun kids TV shows about death."

The feature launched initially on Chromecast with Google TV and TCL Google TV devices, though availability across Roku and Fire TV platforms remains limited. Notably, the voice search does not yet tap into Netflix's personalization engine, as evidenced by the response "We can't answer that one yet, but we're working on it!" when users ask for recommendations based on recent viewing history. All search results appear as written text rather than audio responses.

**Why it matters:** Netflix's proprietary voice search represents a strategic move to reduce dependence on third-party voice assistants and platform operators. By building AI discovery directly into its application, Netflix strengthens its control over the user experience and reduces friction between content discovery and playback. This approach also sidesteps the power dynamics between streaming services and TV OS platforms—a long-standing tension in the streaming industry. As the feature matures and incorporates personalization, it could become a significant competitive advantage for subscriber engagement and retention.

## Google Unveils Fitbit Air: Screenless Fitness Tracker at $100

Google has introduced the Fitbit Air, a minimalist wearable fitness tracker priced at $100 that strips away the display screen entirely to emphasize comfort and simplicity. The device competes in the growing market of screenless fitness bands alongside established players like Whoop, targeting users who find conventional fitness trackers too bulky or complex. The Fitbit Air measures just 5.2 grams without its band and proves 25 percent smaller than the Fitbit Luxe and 50 percent smaller than the Inspire 3 model it succeeds.

The device delivers comprehensive health monitoring capabilities including continuous heart rate tracking, heart rhythm monitoring with atrial fibrillation alerts, blood oxygen level measurement, resting heart rate analysis, heart rate variability calculation, and detailed sleep stage and duration tracking. All data visualization and interaction occurs through the Google Health app on smartphones, allowing the wearable itself to maintain an entirely minimal physical form. Google engineered the screenless design to encourage users to "live in the moment" while still benefiting from 24/7 health monitoring that automatically identifies and tracks common activities and workouts with personalized adaptation over time.

The Fitbit Air offers practical battery performance with up to seven days of operation per charge, plus fast-charging capability that delivers a full day of battery from just five minutes of charging. The device is water-resistant to 50 meters and compatible with the Pixel Watch, enabling users to alternate between wearables depending on context—using the full-featured Pixel Watch during the day and switching to the lighter Fitbit Air for nighttime comfort or workouts. Available pre-order begins immediately with official launch scheduled for May 26, with three band options including a Performance Loop Band from recycled materials, an Active Band for water sports, and an Elevated Modern Band for discrete everyday wear.

**Why it matters:** The Fitbit Air's aggressive pricing and minimalist design challenge the assumption that fitness trackers require screens or premium pricing. By positioning itself as an affordable, comfortable alternative to more feature-rich wearables, Google expands its addressable market beyond users willing to pay $300+ for smartwatches. The integration with the new Google Health platform and Gemini-powered coaching creates a comprehensive ecosystem where the Fitbit Air serves as the data collection layer for more sophisticated AI-powered insights, multiplying the device's value proposition and deepening user engagement within Google's health platform.

## Toronto Police Arrest SMS Blaster Gang in First Canadian Case

Toronto police have arrested three men and filed 44 criminal charges related to the operation of an SMS blaster device that transmitted malicious text messages to tens of thousands of wireless devices across the downtown core. The Toronto Police Service confirmed this represents the "first known instance" of SMS blaster technology being deployed in Canada. The operation, which ran from November 2025 through the spring of 2026, targeted users across multiple districts using a mobile attack platform operated from a vehicle.

SMS blasters exploit security vulnerabilities in aging 2G cellular networks by spoofing cell tower signals and broadcasting stronger transmissions than legitimate infrastructure. When nearby phones and tablets connect to the blaster, the device can transmit thousands of text messages containing links to phishing pages designed to impersonate legitimate businesses. Law enforcement identified that the Toronto operation specifically targeted usernames, passwords, and banking credentials. Detective Sergeant Lindsay Riddell noted during a press conference that such devices also disrupt cellular communications and can interfere with emergency 911 services, creating broader public safety concerns beyond fraud.
