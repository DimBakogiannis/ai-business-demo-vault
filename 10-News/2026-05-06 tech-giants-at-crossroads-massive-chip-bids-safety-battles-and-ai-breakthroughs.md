## Braintrust Confirms Security Breach: AI Platform Demands Customer API Key Rotation

Braintrust, an AI evaluation platform that serves as an "operating system for engineers building AI software," confirmed that hackers obtained unauthorized access to one of its Amazon Web Services cloud environments, prompting the company to instruct all customers to immediately revoke and replace their API keys stored on the platform.

The breach notification, sent to customers via email on Monday and disclosed publicly on Tuesday, acknowledged that the incident occurred but stated that investigators "have not found evidence of broader exposure" beyond one affected customer so far. Nevertheless, Braintrust's cautious posture prompted the blanket directive to every customer to rotate any API keys they maintained through the platform. The company indicated the cause remains under investigation and stated it has contained the incident by locking down the compromised account, restricting access across related systems, and rotating internal secrets.

API keys represent particularly lucrative targets for attackers because these digital credentials grant system access to unauthorized parties without requiring them to breach target networks directly. Once an attacker possesses valid API keys, they can impersonate legitimate users and access services, data, and systems as if they were authorized employees. Jaime Blasco, co-founder of cybersecurity startup Nudge Security, warned that the breach carries "downstream implications for affected customers," particularly AI companies that depend on Braintrust's evaluation infrastructure.

The incident echoes a 2023 breach at CircleCI, another platform serving software developers, where attackers similarly stole API credentials. In that case, CircleCI also instructed customers to rotate "any and all secrets" maintained on its platform. Braintrust spokesperson Martin Bergman told TechCrunch the company issued the email "out of an abundance of caution," though he stated definitively that "there is no evidence of a breach at this time"—a characterization that conflicts somewhat with the company's public acknowledgment of "unauthorized access."

The breach underscores growing security risks for platforms that intermediate AI development and deployment. As companies increasingly concentrate sensitive credentials and AI infrastructure on third-party platforms, those platforms become attractive targets for sophisticated attackers. The incident demonstrates why major cloud tenants require not just robust security practices but also transparent incident communication and rapid remediation capabilities to maintain customer confidence in the AI era.
