# OpenAI's Internal Power Struggle Goes Public: Court Testimony Reveals Allegations of Deception and Abusive Leadership

The courtroom battle between Elon Musk and Sam Altman over control of OpenAI has exposed a startlingly fractious leadership story at one of the world's most influential artificial intelligence companies. As the trial unfolds, testimony from key executives reveals a pattern of alleged deception, questionable management tactics, and a fundamental disagreement over the company's direction that dates back nearly a decade—ultimately reshaping how the public understands the leadership of one of the most important technology firms of our time.

On Wednesday, Mira Murati, OpenAI's former Chief Technology Officer, testified under oath that CEO Sam Altman repeatedly lied to her about critical safety protocols for AI models. In a video deposition shown to the court, Murati stated that Altman falsely claimed OpenAI's legal department had determined that a new AI model did not require review by the company's safety board—a claim she later discovered to be untrue. When asked directly whether Altman was telling the truth, Murati responded simply: "No."

## A Pattern of Alleged Dishonesty

Murati's testimony adds to a growing body of evidence suggesting that Altman's management style has been characterized by inconsistency and misrepresentation. During her deposition, Murati described contacting Jason Kwon, OpenAI's general counsel and current chief strategy officer, to verify Altman's claims about the safety board. "I confirmed that what Jason was saying and what Sam was saying were not the same thing," she testified, emphasizing the "misalignment" between the two executives. To err on the side of caution, Murati ensured the model proceeded through the safety review process despite Altman's statement.

This is far from the first time Altman has faced accusations of dishonesty from inside OpenAI. Cofounder Ilya Sutskever, in a 52-page memo to the board that was read as part of trial testimony, described Altman as exhibiting "a consistent pattern of lying, undermining his execs, and pitting his execs against one another." Former OpenAI board member Helen Toner, in a 2024 podcast discussion about Altman's brief firing in November 2023, said that multiple OpenAI executives had shared evidence with the board of Altman "lying and being manipulative in different situations."

Murati herself corroborated these characterizations during her testimony, agreeing with descriptions of Altman as prone to undermining executives and pitting them against each other. She described her role at OpenAI as incredibly challenging, made more difficult by what she characterized as a lack of clear leadership. "I had an incredibly hard job to do in an organization that was very complex," Murati testified. "I was asking Sam to lead, and lead with clarity, and not undermine my ability to do my job."

When the OpenAI board fired Altman in 2023, they stated that he "was not consistently candid in his communications with the board, hindering its ability to exercise its responsibilities." At the time, the board's action sent shockwaves through the AI industry, though Altman was ultimately reinstated just days later following intense pressure from employees and investors. When asked by The Verge about the board's loss of confidence, Altman deflected, suggesting the question would be "better for them" to answer.

## The Roots of Conflict: A Disagreement Over Control

The tensions between Altman and the company's founders run far deeper than recent management disputes. According to testimony from Greg Brockman, OpenAI's president and cofounder, the conflict traces back to August 2017, when the organization was transitioning from a non-profit research lab to a for-profit entity that could raise capital to pursue artificial general intelligence (AGI).

In what Brockman described as a tense meeting, Elon Musk demanded full control of the newly formed for-profit company. To set a conciliatory tone, Musk had given each cofounder a Tesla Model 3. Ilya Sutskever, OpenAI's head of research, had even commissioned a painting of a Tesla as a friendly gesture for Musk during the meeting. None of it worked.

When Brockman and other executives made clear they would not grant Musk the control he sought, the Tesla and SpaceX founder became visibly angry. "He grabbed the painting and started to storm out of the room," Brockman testified. "And then he turned around and said, 'When will you be departing OpenAI?'" According to Brockman's account, Musk left the meeting, stopped his regular financial donations to OpenAI's operating budget, and within six months, departed the board entirely (though he continued to pay for office space the company shared with Neuralink until 2020).

This pivotal moment set the stage for the company's trajectory. With Musk no longer in the picture, Altman and Brockman consolidated power, eventually building OpenAI into the dominant force in AI that it is today. Yet the original disagreement about the company's direction and governance structure has apparently never fully been resolved—it simply festered for years, ultimately exploding into the lawsuit that now plays out in public courtrooms.
