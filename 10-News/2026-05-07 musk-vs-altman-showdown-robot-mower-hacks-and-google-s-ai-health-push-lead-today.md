## Yarbo Robot Lawn Mowers Exposed to Catastrophic Security Vulnerabilities

Security researcher Andreas Makris has disclosed a critical vulnerability in Yarbo robot lawn mowers that allows remote attackers to take complete control of thousands of devices from anywhere in the world. The $5,000 autonomous yard robots, which can be configured with different attachments for mowing, snow removal, or trimming, expose a chilling security gap: hackers can hijack the bladed machines, override safety mechanisms, and manipulate them with impunity. Makris demonstrated the severity by remotely controlling a Yarbo robot and moving it toward The Verge's Sean Hollister, who deliberately lay in the machine's path to document the vulnerability.

According to The Verge's investigation, the vulnerability stems from multiple foundational security failures in Yarbo's design. Each robot runs Linux with a hardcoded root password that remains identical across all devices, effectively giving attackers a master key to the entire fleet. The robots communicate through unsecured MQTT protocols, meaning that once a hacker gains access to a single device, they can theoretically command every Yarbo robot worldwide. Makris has catalogued over 11,000 Yarbo robots globally, with approximately 5,400 units operating in the United States and Europe. This means a single attacker potentially controls thousands of bladed yard robots scattered across residential neighborhoods and commercial properties.

The implications extend beyond individual device hijacking. Security researchers point out that attackers could disable the emergency stop button through remote commands, leaving homeowners unable to regain physical control of a runaway machine. Additionally, because Yarbo robots function as full Linux computers, hackers could reprogram them to execute arbitrary commands, turn them into botnets for distributed attacks, or use them to probe home networks for sensitive information. Founded in 2015, Yarbo initially focused on snow removal robots before expanding into multi-purpose yard automation, but the company has not publicly disclosed any plans to address these critical vulnerabilities. The incident echoes previous IoT security disasters and underscores how rapidly proliferating smart devices often ship without adequate security testing or hardening.
