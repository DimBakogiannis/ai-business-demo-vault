## Google Launches $9.99 Monthly AI Health Coach Powered by Gemini

Google has officially entered the AI-driven personal coaching space with a new subscription service launching May 19. The company announced that its Google Health app—a rebranding of the longstanding Fitbit application—will now include an AI-powered health coach built on its Gemini AI model, priced at $9.99 monthly or $99 annually as part of a Google Health Premium subscription. Subscribers to Google's higher-tier AI services (AI Pro and Ultra) will gain access without additional cost.

The health coach functions as a comprehensive fitness trainer, sleep specialist, and wellness advisor rolled into one digital assistant. Unlike generic fitness apps, Google has designed the coach to learn user preferences through an initial onboarding questionnaire that captures health goals, daily routines, available exercise equipment, existing injuries, and lifestyle factors. Once calibrated, the coach leverages this personal context to deliver customized workout suggestions, sleep analysis, and integrated health insights drawn from multiple data sources including fitness metrics, sleep logs, nutrition tracking, environmental factors, and—with proper permissions—U.S. medical records.

The service distinguishes itself by synthesizing information across multiple domains rather than presenting isolated summaries. The health coach functionality extends beyond a dedicated dashboard; Google has woven AI coaching into various app sections including the Fitness tab's workout suggestions, the Sleep tab's tracking analysis, and the Health tab's metric summaries. Users can interact conversationally with the coach using natural language to adjust goals, log workouts and meals through voice dictation, or upload photos and files for analysis.

Initially, the health coach will be available exclusively to Fitbit and Pixel Watch users, with support for other devices promised in the future. This move represents a significant expansion of Google's fitness ecosystem strategy, which began with its 2021 acquisition of Fitbit. By bundling AI coaching with hardware and data analytics, Google aims to create stickier engagement within its health and wellness platform, potentially competing with specialized services like Apple Health and other fitness-focused ecosystems.

## Google Introduces Fitbit Air: A Minimalist $100 Wearable

Complementing its software expansion, Google unveiled the Fitbit Air, a screenless health tracker priced at $100 that targets users seeking simplicity and comfort over feature density. The device represents a departure from traditional smartwatch design, dispensing with a display entirely to encourage what Google describes as living "in the moment" while still capturing comprehensive health metrics.

The Fitbit Air packs substantial functionality into a remarkably compact form factor. At just 12 grams with its band and 5.2 grams without, the device is 25 percent smaller than the Fitbit Luxe and half the size of the Inspire 3. Despite its diminutive profile, it delivers continuous heart rate monitoring, heart rhythm tracking with atrial fibrillation detection, blood oxygen measurement, resting heart rate calculation, and heart rate variability analysis alongside automated sleep staging and duration tracking. The device intelligently recognizes common activities and workouts, with personalization improving as it learns user patterns over time.

Battery performance remains competitive with premium wearables: users can expect up to one week of continuous wear, with fast charging delivering a full day of usage in just five minutes. The Air maintains water resistance to 50 meters and integrates with Google's Pixel Watch ecosystem, allowing users to pair both devices for complementary use cases—the larger Pixel Watch for daytime functionality and the lighter Fitbit Air for evening wear or focused workouts.

The device launches in three band variants: a Performance Loop Band made from recycled materials with breathable weave, an Active Band designed for water sports, and an Elevated Modern Band for discrete styling. Available for pre-order immediately with a May 26 retail launch, the Fitbit Air positions itself against premium competitors like Whoop while maintaining a considerably lower price point. This move solidifies Google's vision of offering a tiered hardware ecosystem that caters to various user preferences while funneling data into its unified Google Health platform.

## Netflix Tests Native AI Voice Search Without Third-Party Assistants

Netflix has begun testing an AI-powered native voice search feature that lets subscribers ask for content recommendations directly through their remote controls without relying on Google Assistant, Amazon Alexa, or other third-party voice systems. The feature, currently available to a limited subset of U.S. users on select devices, represents a strategic move to maintain direct control over the discovery experience and potentially sidestep platform fees associated with voice assistant integrations.

The voice search interface presents users with preset suggestions upon activation—phrases like "I need a good cry," "watch in the background," and "help me stay awake"—each leading to curated recommendations. However, the feature's strength lies in its "Ask" function, which accepts natural language queries and returns contextually appropriate suggestions. Early testers discovered the system impressed with both obvious requests and highly specific queries. Standard searches like "date night movies from the '80s" returned classics such as The Breakfast Club, while more esoteric requests like "fun kids TV shows about death" correctly surfaced A Series of Unfortunate Events and Raising Dion. Even abstract emotional queries—"I had too much coffee today, what should I watch"—generated sensible recommendations for comedy specials and relaxation content.

The feature currently rolls out on Google TV-based devices like Chromecast with Google TV and TCL televisions, though availability varies even within the same ecosystem. Notably, the functionality isn't yet available on Roku or Fire TV platforms, likely due to platform negotiations and technical integration challenges. The voice search operates independently of Netflix's personalization engine at present, with the app acknowledging limitations in some areas ("We can't answer that one yet, but we're working on it!").

This development illuminates broader power dynamics within streaming and television platforms. By developing its own voice discovery layer, Netflix circumvents potential platform fees and maintains control over how its catalog is surfaced to users. Rather than relying on Gemini or Alexa to interpret and route voice queries, Netflix intercepts the interaction at the app level, strengthening its relationship with subscribers while reducing dependence on external voice assistants. The rollout suggests Netflix may prioritize expanding this feature to additional platforms and devices as development progresses.

## Toronto Police Dismantle First Known Canadian SMS Blaster Operation

Canadian law enforcement has arrested three individuals and filed 44 charges related to operating an SMS blaster—a cellular spoofing device that authorities describe as the first known instance of such technology being deployed in Canada. Beginning in November 2025, the operation targeted tens of thousands of devices across downtown Toronto with malicious text messages designed to harvest login credentials and banking information through phishing schemes.

SMS blasters exploit fundamental security vulnerabilities in aging 2G cellular networks by broadcasting artificially strong signals that override legitimate towers and force nearby phones and tablets to connect to the rogue device. Once established, the blaster transmits thousands of messages containing links to fraudulent login pages impersonating legitimate financial institutions and services. The Toronto police indicated the device was "uniquely built" and operated from the back of a vehicle, enabling deployment across multiple locations to affect different neighborhoods.

Beyond credential theft, the blaster poses direct threats to public safety. Authorities emphasized that such devices interfere with standard cellular communications and can disrupt emergency 911 services during deployment. The operation mirrors similar incidents globally—Thai police arrested gang members in 2024 for operating an SMS blaster from a Bangkok truck that dispatched nearly one million messages over a three-day window, demonstrating this technique's effectiveness as a crime tool.

Users can defend against SMS blaster attacks through relatively simple measures. Disabling 2G cellular connectivity eliminates the primary vulnerability these devices exploit, as they depend on older network protocols for their spoofing technique.
