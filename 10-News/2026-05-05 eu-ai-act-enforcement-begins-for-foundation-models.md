# EU AI Act Enforcement Begins for Foundation Models

> [!summary] TL;DR
> The first wave of [[EU AI Act]] obligations took effect today, requiring providers of general-purpose AI models to publish training data summaries, copyright compliance policies, and serious-incident reports. Affects [[OpenAI]], [[Anthropic]], [[Google]], [[Meta]], and others operating in the EU.

## Key Concepts
[[EU AI Act]], [[AI Regulation]], [[AI Ethics]], [[OpenAI]], [[Anthropic]], [[Google]], [[Meta]], [[Open Source AI]]

## Summary
Article 53 of the [[EU AI Act]] now imposes documentation and transparency requirements on all foundation model providers serving EU users. Open-weight model distributors — including [[Meta]] (for [[Llama]]) and [[Mistral]] — receive partial exemptions but must still publish training data summaries.

[[OpenAI]] and [[Anthropic]] both confirmed compliance in advance. The European AI Office now has the authority to request internal evaluations and impose fines up to 3% of global annual turnover.

## Why It Matters
This is the first jurisdiction to attach binding obligations to foundation models themselves, not just downstream applications. The compliance template established here will likely shape upcoming regulation in the UK, Canada, and parts of APAC. For [[AI in Business]] teams, expect new procurement questionnaires from EU customers.
